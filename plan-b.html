What changed from Rev 6 — front half only
  1. A second exterior door. The laundry becomes a real mud room with its own door from the driveway. Boots, feed, groceries and wet dogs stop coming through the front hall. This is the change that matters most day to day, and it costs one door.
  2. The bedroom wing widened a foot. Bedrooms 2 and 3 go from 11′-0″ to 12′-0″ clear — 129 and 121 sf instead of 118 and 111. A 12-foot room takes a queen with nightstands on both sides; an 11-foot one does not.
  3. The master gives up that foot. 14′-1″ instead of 15′-1″, still 7′-7″ of spare width around a king. Three usable bedrooms beats one generous one and two tight ones.
  4. The washer and dryer moved to the mud room’s north wall so the south wall is free for the door, with the bench, water heater and linen tower wrapping the corner.
  5. Bath 2 tightened 6 inches to give the mud room the depth it needed. Still a full 5′ tub, vanity and toilet.
  6. Everything in the back half is byte-identical — glass wall, sliders, kitchen, island, dining, living, stair, and all the corner windows.

North Elevation — The Glass Wall

This wall no longer braces the building

8′-0″ of solid wall in 40′-0″, and every piece of it is a post: two 1′-6″ corner returns, three 1′-0″ posts, and two 1′-0″ slivers between the window pairs. There is no braced wall panel left here. All of the north–south shear has to come off the east and west walls and the roof diaphragm, and an engineer has to say so on paper before anyone frames it.

Header deflection is the thing that fails

Multi-slide makers cap header deflection at L/600, or ⅛″, whichever is less — far stiffer than the L/240 code asks for. Over a 12-foot opening that is the difference between doors that glide in year five and doors that bind once the roof loads up. Get the header sized to the door supplier's number.

Everything lines up at 80″

Door heads, the 24″×72″ verticals, the high strips, every window in the house — one head height, only the sills move. The verticals sill out at 8″, which is what makes the band read as continuous rather than as punched openings.

All of it is safety glazing

An 8″ sill puts every vertical inside the 18″-of-floor rule, and the sliders are glazed doors. Tempered throughout this wall, no exceptions and no value-engineering it out.

Build Notes — This Site

Lateral bracing has to be solved on paper

Between the glass wall on the north and the stair opening in the floor above, this building has very little wall left to brace itself. The east and west walls now carry it. Get that engineered before the post layout is ordered — it is the one item on this sheet that can force the plan to change.

The stair changes the structure

A second storey means a real floor system and load paths this post-frame shell does not have. Decide how far the upper level reaches: over the bedroom wing only, leaving the great room open to the ridge, or across the whole footprint. That answer sets the columns, the footings, and whether the north wall can stay a non-bearing gable end.

The kitchen sink lost its window

The east-wall trio occupies the first 9 feet of that wall, which is where the sink run was. The run is now 5′-2″ starting south of the windows, and the sink sits under upper cabinets instead of glass, with the corner windows two feet to its left. If a window over the sink matters more than the third vertical, say so and W16 comes out.

Floor-level glass on the uphill side

Thirteen windows now sill at 8″, most of them on the uphill north and side walls. Set the slab edge high, cut the bank back, and run the swale and French drain along the north side before anything else goes in.

Recess the slab at D1 and D2

Multi-slide units want a low or flush sill, and that recess cannot be added after the pour. Two 12-foot recesses, dead flat, formed with the thickened edge.

Glazing ratio

24% window-to-floor. That will not pass on the prescriptive path — plan on the performance or UA-alternative route, and put the money into the two slider units where 160 sf of the 388 sits.
